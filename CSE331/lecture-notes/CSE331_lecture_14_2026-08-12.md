# CSE331 — Lecture 14: Designing CFGs — More Worked Examples, CFL Closure Properties

Continues Topic 10 (CFG design) from Lectures 12–13. Moves from single-alphabet regular/CFL examples into: (1) a case-annotated design for a classic non-regular language ("more 1s than 0s"), (2) exactly-k-occurrence languages via a generic Σ* variable, (3) a systematic two-block ($X$/$Y$ or $A$/$B$) technique for three-terminal languages with linear exponent relations ($a^ib^jc^k$ family), and (4) closure properties of CFLs, including the standard proof that CFLs are **not** closed under intersection.

## $\{\omega : \omega \text{ has more 1s than 0s}\}$

$$S \to 0S1 \mid 1S0 \mid 1S \mid SS \mid 1$$

Board annotations under each alternative (arrows pointing from production to caption):

- $0S1$ — handles strings that start with 0 and end with 1.
- $1S0$ — opposite case (start with 1, end with 0).
- $1S$ — adds an extra leading 1; allows multiple 1s consecutively.
- $SS$ — handles [strings that] start and end with the same symbol: concatenates two independently-valid substrings, covering the case $0S1$/$1S0$ miss (first and last symbols both 0, or both 1).
- $1$ — base case; ensures at least one more 1 than 0 (the "extra" 1 that guarantees the count imbalance).

## $\{\omega : \omega \text{ contains at least three 1s}\}$

Regex form: $\Sigma^*1\Sigma^*1\Sigma^*1\Sigma^*$, with $A$ standing in for $\Sigma^*$:

$$A \to 0A \mid 1A \mid \varepsilon$$
$$S \to A1A1A1A$$

$A$ generates arbitrary padding; the three explicit 1s in $S$'s production guarantee at least three 1s regardless of what $A$ contributes.

## Even palindrome (recap)

$$\{\omega : \omega = \omega^R, |\omega| \text{ is even}\}$$
$$P \to 0P0 \mid 1P1 \mid \varepsilon$$

## $\{\omega : |\omega| \text{ is odd, middle symbol is } 0\}$

$$S \to 0S0 \mid 1S1 \mid 1S0 \mid 0S1 \mid 0$$

Not a palindrome constraint — the two outer symbols added at each recursive step are **not** required to match (all four combinations $0S0, 1S1, 1S0, 0S1$ are present), only the base case is fixed. Since every derivation bottoms out at $S \to 0$, the single middle character is always 0 regardless of how the outer symbols were chosen at each step.

## Three-terminal languages ($a^ib^jc^k$ family) — systematic two-block construction

Six variants worked in sequence, each via the same $X/Y$ (or $A/B$) two-block pattern: one block ties two exponents together with a linear relation, the other pads the third.

**$\{a^ib^jc^k : i = k\}$**
$$X \to \varepsilon \mid aXc \mid Y$$
$$Y \to bY \mid \varepsilon$$

**$\{a^ib^jc^k : i = j\}$** (labeled $A$ under $a^i$, $B$ under $b^j$)
$$S \to AB$$
$$A \to \varepsilon \mid aAb$$
$$B \to \varepsilon \mid cB$$

**$\{a^ib^jc^k : i+j = k\}$**
$$X \to \varepsilon \mid aXc \mid Y$$
$$Y \to \varepsilon \mid bYc$$

**$\{a^ib^jc^k : i+k = j\}$**
$$S \to AB$$
$$A \to \varepsilon \mid aAb$$
$$B \to \varepsilon \mid bBc$$

**$\{a^ib^jc^k : 2i+j = k\}$**
$$X \to \varepsilon \mid aXcc \mid Y$$
$$Y \to \varepsilon \mid bYc$$

**$\{a^ib^jc^k : i+2k = j\}$**
$$S \to AB$$
$$A \to \varepsilon \mid aAb$$
$$B \to \varepsilon \mid bbBc$$

> [!note] Pattern
> In every variant, one block wraps a pair of terminals around a recursive call to enforce a linear tie between two exponents (doubling a terminal count on one side doubles its coefficient in the relation, e.g. $aXcc$ / $bbBc$ give the factor-2 terms), and the other block independently pads the third, unconstrained terminal. Reading the exponent relation off the grammar (or vice versa) is the generalizable skill here, not memorizing six separate grammars.

## CFL closure properties

Context-free languages are **closed under**: concatenation, union, star.
Context-free languages are **not closed under intersection.**

**Counterexample (standard proof):**

$$\{a^ib^jc^k : i=j\} \cap \{a^ib^jc^k : j=k\} = \{a^ib^jc^k : i=j=k\}$$

The right-hand side is the classic non-context-free language (pumping-lemma failure case), so the intersection of two CFLs here is not itself a CFL — proving CFLs are not closed under intersection.
