# Exam Questionnaire Preparation Strategy of CSE321

Standard guidelines for CSE321 exam question difficulty distribution (Easy 35%, Moderate 35%, Difficult 30% with 10% Advanced), question classifications, and difficulty definitions.
Docx last modified: 07-06-2026.

## Content

### Overview & Standards
- **Version**: Summer 2026 Version
- **Scope**: Standard for both Midterm and Final exam questionnaire preparation across all CSE 321 sections.

### Points Distribution by Difficulty Level
- **Easy (E)**: 35% of total points.
- **Moderate (M)**: 35% of total points.
- **Difficult (D)**: 30% of total points.
  - Among difficult questions, **10%** will fall into the **Advanced (A)** category.
- **Question Tagging Format**: All questions will include category and Course Outcome markers: `CO${CO-number}-CA${category-initial}`.

### Question Categories & Ratio Breakdown
Due to the mathematical imbalance between Midterm (mostly conceptual/theoretical) and Final (mathematical topics in most post-midterm chapters), questions are grouped into six classes:
1. Theoretical Questions
2. Problem Solving Questions
3. Program Interpretation
4. Error Detection/Correction
5. Mathematical Calculation
6. Simulation Questions

- **Questionnaire Ratio**:
  - **Theoretical Questions**: 35% to 50%
  - **Other Classes (Problem Solving, Code, Math, Simulation)**: 50% to 65%

### Difficulty Level Assessment Criteria
- **Easy Question**:
  - Requires remembering material discussed directly in class or presented on slides.
  - *Theory*: Rote memorization of definitions or processes.
  - *Simulation / Problem Solving*: Very similar to examples in class, slides, or practice sheets.
  - *Note*: Lengthy simulations remain "easy" if understanding what is asked is straightforward.
- **Moderate Question**:
  - Requires textbook reading to understand rationales behind concepts (pros/cons, implementation mechanisms, design tradeoffs).
  - *Problem Solving*: Interpreting algorithmic program outputs or solving computational problems where underlying elements are familiar but application requires multi-step thought.
  - *Simulation*: Simulating OS behavior whose logic was discussed and explained in the textbook, even if that specific simulation was not shown in class.
- **Difficult Question**:
  - Tests deep conceptual grasp of operating systems in general.
  - *Theory*: Answers cannot be found directly in the textbook; they must be derived from underlying principles.
  - *Problem Solving*: Detecting and fixing subtle programming bugs, or evaluating consequences of alternative/non-standard OS design and implementation choices.
